# After the proof — finishing the archive

The seven-scene presentation is the shareable baseline. Keep it concise until Coodie has seen it:

`door → Earth → Chicago → one record → official air → CC-0217 → 1994–2026`

The proof is intentionally an argument, not a complete product tour. The ideas removed from presentation mode are still available in the product and should return only when their content and interaction quality justify the time they ask from a viewer.

## First: get the truth from the archive

Before deepening any interface, ask for:

- The actual catalog scope and approved language for its scale. Do not publish an hour count without confirmation.
- Five to ten real tape labels or a redacted shot-log sample.
- A small set of approved stills or clips representing different eras, cities, people, and formats.
- Real Creative Control Television show names, channel language, idents, and programming groupings.
- Confirmed dates for Channel Zero, the first Ye footage, and other timeline anchors.
- Public, members-only, restricted, and never-public rules.
- The cities and travel arcs Coodie considers essential to the story.

## Phase 1 — replace the mock

This is the highest-value work after the meeting.

1. Replace prototype frames with approved stills and footage.
2. Replace invented or generic tape metadata with real labels and provenance.
3. Rewrite timeline anchors from confirmed dates and Coodie’s language.
4. Program the TV surface with real shows, eras, and editorial groupings.
5. Establish a rights vocabulary that can be trusted internally and explained publicly.

## Phase 2 — deepen the four exploratory lenses

### World

- Keep the globe as the cinematic overview.
- Turn cities into authored story clusters rather than isolated markers.
- Add real travel arcs only where the archive supports them.
- Refine the fly-to camera, labels, and keyboard-accessible map alternative.
- Restore more locations to the presentation only when each opens a meaningful story.

### Television

- Create a real channel and programming bible with Coodie and Chike.
- Design distinct idents, transitions, schedules, and era blocks from approved material.
- Make remote-style navigation legible without turning the surface into a streaming dashboard.
- Preserve CH 07 as the public-network proof; restore Studio and other channels after their identity is genuinely different.

### Tapes

- Replace the CSS camera with a deliberate optical or 3D aisle interaction.
- Introduce real spine labels, shelf locations, contact sheets, and digitization status.
- Let a tape reveal its descendants: clips, people, places, projects, transcripts, and rights.
- Preserve CC-0217 as the proof tape; restore the full aisle when navigation feels physical and precise.

### Timeline

- Author a small number of meaningful era chapters instead of filling every year.
- Connect each date to people, places, tapes, projects, and public footage.
- Make gaps explicit; absence is part of an archive and should not look like broken UI.
- Restore deeper year, month, and day navigation once the source dates are verified.

## Phase 3 — make the archive operational

- Build an ingest workflow for tapes, clips, stills, transcripts, tags, and corrections.
- Add transcript search, speaker identification, and semantic discovery with human review.
- Add internal permissions, rights review, redaction, embargoes, and publish scheduling.
- Separate public storytelling from the private working archive while keeping one data model.
- Add durable storage, backups, derivatives, checksums, and preservation metadata.
- Define editorial ownership so the site can keep changing after launch.

## Before a public launch

- Test the core flows on phones, tablets, laptops, reduced motion, and slower connections.
- Complete keyboard, screen-reader, focus, contrast, and caption testing.
- Optimize the globe, broadcast media, thumbnails, and route payloads.
- Add analytics that measure discovery without turning the archive into a growth dashboard.
- Write clear prototype, public, restricted, and archival-source labels.
- Run a final content and rights review with Coodie’s team.

## Material intentionally cut from the pitch

These routes are not deleted. They are simply outside the seven-scene walk:

- The empty “never aired” clip proof
- The broad Ye-only archive query
- The Studio television variation
- The unselected tape aisle
- Dense timeline year and through-line views
- Collections, entity pages, and constellation exploration

They can become strong supporting rooms later. Do not lengthen the pitch to prove they exist.
